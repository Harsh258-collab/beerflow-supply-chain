# BeerFlow — Key Findings & Insights

**Author:** Harsh Raj Pandey  
**Date:** September 2026  
**Scope:** Beverage Supply Chain Analysis | India | Jan 2023 – Dec 2024

---

## Finding 1: Seasonal Demand Mismatch Is the Biggest Revenue Risk

The data shows demand spikes of **30–70% above baseline** during:
- October–December (Diwali, Christmas, New Year)
- March–April (Summer onset)

But current inventory and ordering patterns don't adjust for this. The result is predictable stockouts at exactly the moments when customers are most willing to buy.

**The fix is straightforward:** Build seasonal inventory buffers by late September each year. A 25% buffer on top SKUs (Lager 500ml, Strong Beer 500ml) would be sufficient based on historical variance.

---

## Finding 2: East India Is the Weak Link in the Distribution Network

Average lead time in East India: **6.2 days**  
Average lead time in North/West India: **2.8 days**

That's a 2.2x difference — and it's not geography alone. The fragmented distribution structure (multiple small truckers, no consolidation hub) is the primary culprit. Goods arrive late, OTIF suffers, and distributors in the region underperform despite not being the root cause.

**The fix:** A dedicated consolidation hub in Kolkata + rail freight contracts for bulk SKUs. Rail is 3x cheaper than road trucking per case for distances above 800km.

---

## Finding 3: 40% of Distributors Are Underperforming — And We Know Who

The weighted scorecard (OTIF 35%, Fill Rate 30%, Damage 20%, TAT 15%) puts 40% of distributors in Grade C or D. These aren't random failures — they cluster in:
- East India (infrastructure constraints)
- Central India (training & process gaps)

**The fix:** Quarterly business reviews, digital order management tools, and a tiered incentive scheme that rewards Grade A distributors with better margins and priority allocation.

---

## Finding 4: We're Over-Ordering the Wrong SKUs

Stout 650ml is consistently ordered **45% above EOQ** across warehouses. This ties up working capital unnecessarily and increases holding costs by an estimated INR 8–12 Lakhs annually.

Meanwhile, Lager 500ml — the highest-volume SKU — shows near-optimal ordering behavior and can serve as an internal benchmark.

**The fix:** Implement EOQ-guided ordering for the bottom 3 SKUs. This alone could free up INR 8–12L in working capital annually.

---

## Finding 5: Rail Freight Is Criminally Underutilized

Rail freight usage: **15%**  
Road (truck) usage: **55%**

For bulk shipments above 500 cases going more than 800km, rail is significantly cheaper. Yet 85% of orders still go by road. This is partly inertia, partly a lack of formal freight modal analysis.

**The fix:** Negotiate annual rail freight contracts for bulk Lager/Strong Beer shipments to East and Central India. Projecting savings of INR 15–20L annually at 25% rail utilization.
